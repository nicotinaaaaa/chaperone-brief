This brief covers biology, biotech and pharma news from roughly the past two weeks, weighted toward Oct 18–25 because the Oct 18 brief already covered Oct 8–18 (BMS admilparant, BioNTech BNT113, argenx, Arrowhead tau drug, Entrada, Revolution Medicines, City/Iambic IPOs are not repeated). **Coverage note:** many trade-press sites block automated access and several articles gave no numbers or stock moves. I include only details I could confirm and say where something was not available.

**Terms used throughout.** A *clinical trial* tests a drug in people: **Phase 1** (small group, mainly safety), **Phase 2** (early evidence it works), **Phase 3** (large, usually compared with placebo or standard care; the main basis for approval). A *primary endpoint* is the single main measurement a trial is designed to prove; a *secondary endpoint* is an additional measurement. *Placebo* is a dummy treatment. *Statistically significant* means the difference is unlikely to be down to chance. A *hazard ratio (HR)* compares the rate of an event (such as cancer coming back) between two groups over time: 0.58 means 42% fewer events than the comparison group. *Recurrence-free survival (RFS)* is how long patients live without their cancer returning. A *checkpoint inhibitor* (such as Keytruda) is an antibody that removes a "brake" (PD-1) cancer uses to hide from immune cells. The *FDA* is the U.S. drug regulator. An *NDA* (new drug application) is the formal request to the FDA to approve a drug. *Upfront payment* and *milestones* in a deal are, respectively, money paid at signing and money paid later only if goals (trial results, approvals, sales) are met.

## 1. Clinical trials

### First positive Phase 3 for a personalized cancer vaccine: Merck/Moderna's intismeran cuts melanoma relapse risk 42% — but shares fall
**What happened.** On Oct 22, Merck and Moderna announced full results of the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial, presented at the ESMO 2026 cancer congress (ESMO is the European Society for Medical Oncology's annual meeting) and accepted by the *New England Journal of Medicine*. In 1,137 patients whose melanoma had been surgically removed, adding the personalized vaccine intismeran to Keytruda reduced the risk of recurrence or death by 42% (HR 0.58; 95% confidence interval 0.45–0.75, a range showing how precise the estimate is; p<0.001) versus Keytruda plus placebo, after a median follow-up of 24.6 months. Two years out, 82.2% of vaccine patients were recurrence-free versus 70.0%. Risk of the cancer spreading to distant organs fell 38%. There was no difference in the first six months; benefit appeared afterward. Overall survival is not yet mature. Serious (grade 3–5) side effects were similar (14.2% vs 13.4%); common ones were fever, injection-site pain and fatigue. Stock: BioPharma Dive's Oct 22 headline said both Moderna and Merck shares **fell** ("investors sold the news"); I could not retrieve the size of the drop.

**Biology.** Melanoma is skin cancer arising from pigment-producing cells; stage IIB–IV means a thick or already-spreading tumor with a high chance of returning after surgery. Intismeran is an *individualized neoantigen therapy*: doctors sequence each patient's tumor to find mutated proteins ("neoantigens") that normal cells lack, then make a custom *mRNA* (a temporary genetic "recipe" cells read to make a protein) coding for up to 34 of them, so the immune system learns to attack the tumor. Pairing it with Keytruda keeps immune cells switched on.

**Why it matters.** It is the first late-stage proof that a personalized mRNA cancer vaccine works, after the BioNTech BNT113 miss and other mRNA cancer setbacks covered earlier. Researchers quoted by STAT say the approach may extend to other tumors. The share drop shows high expectations were already priced in (Moderna had rallied sharply after the August interim announcement).
**Sources:** [Merck release (Oct 22, 2026)](https://www.merck.com/news/merck-and-moderna-will-present-results-from-landmark-phase-3-interpath-001-trial-for-intismeran-autogene-plus-keytruda-pembrolizumab-at-esmo-2026-presidential-symposium/); [STAT (Oct 25)](https://statnews.com/2026/10/25/mrna-cancer-vaccine-merck-moderna-esmo-intismeran-next-applications); [BioPharma Dive headlines](https://www.biopharmadive.com/topic/clinical-trials/). **Stage:** Phase 3, positive; market-relevant.

### Roivant's brepocitinib wins two Phase 3 trials in a blinding eye inflammation
**What happened.** On Oct 21, Roivant (via its subsidiary Priovant) said brepocitinib met all goals in two Phase 3 studies (CLARITY-1 and -2) in non-infectious uveitis. Median time to "treatment failure" (inflammation or other markers worsening) was over 48 weeks on the drug versus 12 weeks on placebo; patients stayed off steroid immunosuppression for 40 weeks; and fluid swelling in the retina's center improved. Priovant plans to file for approval in the first half of 2027. Analysts at Cantor Fitzgerald estimate up to $5 billion peak sales for this use; Stifel called it potentially "best-in-disease." Share-price movement was not reported in the source.

**Biology.** Non-infectious uveitis is inflammation inside the eye (the uvea, the middle layer) caused by the immune system rather than infection; it brings pain, blurred vision and floaters and can cause blindness if untreated. It often strikes working-age adults and usually needs steroids, which have serious long-term side effects. Brepocitinib is a *TYK2/JAK1 inhibitor*: a pill that blocks two enzymes (TYK2 and JAK1) that relay inflammatory signals inside immune cells. The drug is already FDA-approved (as Lisraya) for dermatomyositis, an autoimmune disease causing muscle weakness and skin rash.
**Why it matters.** A second approved use would broaden the drug and give uveitis patients a steroid-sparing option.
**Sources:** [BioPharma Dive (Oct 21, 2026)](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/roivant-brepocitinib-uveitis-study-results/833569/). **Stage:** Phase 3, positive; market-relevant.

### Disc Medicine's bitopertin succeeds in Phase 3 — shares up roughly 39–60%
**What happened.** On Oct 19, Disc announced the 183-patient APOLLO Phase 3 trial met all primary and secondary goals. Bitopertin lowered blood protoporphyrin IX (a harmful pigment precursor) by 41.4% versus placebo and added 12.2 hours of pain-free time in light per month by month six; phototoxic reactions fell significantly. Dizziness (50% of treated patients) was the most common side effect, mostly mild. Disc plans to resubmit its NDA by end of 2026, with an FDA decision possible mid-2027. Shares rose about 60% in one report and 38.9% on the day in another listing (likely different points in the session).

**Biology.** Erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP) is a rare inherited disease in which the body's production of heme (the iron-carrying part of blood) goes wrong and protoporphyrin IX builds up in red cells and skin; sunlight then triggers severe burning pain, often within minutes, so patients avoid daylight from childhood. Bitopertin is a *GlyT1 inhibitor*: it blocks a transporter protein (GlyT1) that feeds glycine into red-cell heme production, reducing the buildup at its source.
**Why it matters.** It would be a pill addressing the cause rather than only protecting the skin; the FDA had previously not approved it, so this is a second attempt.
**Sources:** [Finviz/Disc release coverage (Oct 19, 2026)](https://finviz.com/news/403992/disc-medicine-shares-rise-60-after-phase-3-bitopertin-trial-meets-primary-and-secondary-endpoints); [HCPLive](https://www.hcplive.com/view/bitopertin-meets-all-endpoints-phase-3-apollo-trial-epp). **Stage:** Phase 3, positive; market-relevant.

### Rapport's epilepsy drug RAP-219 shows modest but real effect in bipolar mania
**What happened.** On Oct 20, Rapport reported a Phase 2 bipolar mania trial in which RAP-219 improved the Young Mania Rating Scale (YMRS, an 11-item clinician score of manic symptoms) by 3.1 points more than placebo at week 3 (statistically significant). That fell short of the 4-point bar the CEO had called clinically meaningful, though existing drugs show 4–7 points. All 11 items improved. Rapport plans Phase 3 bipolar trials next year. Tikr reported shares up about 4%.

**Biology.** Bipolar disorder causes swings between depression and mania — days of abnormally high energy, little sleep, racing thoughts and risky behavior. RAP-219 is a small molecule that blocks *TARPγ8*, a helper protein attached to AMPA receptors (docking sites for the excitatory brain chemical glutamate) found mainly in the hippocampus and cortex, thereby calming overactive circuits. It is already in Phase 3 for focal seizures (seizures starting in one brain region).
**Why it matters.** It is the first new mechanism for mania in decades, but the effect-size debate and unanswered safety questions (e.g. suicidality was not mentioned) matter.
**Sources:** [Fierce Biotech (Oct 20, 2026)](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/rapport-bipolar-data-strike-chord-analysts-despite-missing-target); [Tikr](https://www.tikr.com/blog/rapport-therapeutics-gains-4-on-positive-bipolar-mania-trial-results-first-new-mechanism-in-decades). **Stage:** Phase 2, proof of concept; market-relevant.

### Oncology early data and a Phase 3 win (grouped)
- **Verastem's VS-7375 in pancreatic cancer (Oct 14).** An oral drug blocking KRAS G12D (a mutated version of the growth-signal protein KRAS found in many pancreatic cancers) produced responses in 4 of 8 second-line patients and 41% across 22 pretreated patients; a Phase 3 first-line trial should start before end of 2026. Pancreatic cancer is usually found late and is among the deadliest cancers. *Source:* [Labiotech](https://www.labiotech.eu/trends-news/oncology-news-october-2026/). **Stage:** Phase 1/2, early.
- **BMS's RYZ101 (Oct 12).** An actinium-225 radiopharmaceutical (a drug that delivers a radioactive atom directly to tumor cells) met its Phase 3 goal of improving progression-free survival (time without tumor growth) in gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (slow-growing tumors of hormone-producing cells in the gut and pancreas). No numbers released. *Source:* Labiotech. **Stage:** Phase 3, topline.

## 2. Regulatory decisions

- **GSK/Ionis' Hibsago (bepirovirsen) — FDA approval for chronic hepatitis B (Oct 20).** Hepatitis B is a liver virus that, when chronic, can cause cirrhosis (scarring) and liver cancer; current pills suppress but rarely clear it. Hibsago is an *antisense oligonucleotide* (a short strand of genetic material that blocks production of a specific protein or viral product) and also stimulates the immune response. Added to standard antivirals for six months of injections (most self-administered), it produced a "functional cure" (no detectable virus for 24 weeks off treatment) in about one-fifth of patients versus none on placebo in two Phase 3 studies published in NEJM. It is the first antisense therapy approved for hepatitis B. The label warns of liver-enzyme spikes and low platelets, requiring frequent blood tests, which Jefferies said may slow uptake; Jefferies estimates peak sales near $3 billion. Price not yet disclosed; no stock moves reported. *Source:* [BioPharma Dive (Oct 21)](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/gsk-ionis-fda-approve-hibsago-chronic-hepatitis-b-bepirovirsen/833541/).
- **Pfizer's Tukysa — FDA approval as maintenance therapy for HER2-positive breast cancer (Oct 7).** HER2-positive breast cancer is driven by excess HER2, a growth-signal protein. In 654 patients, median time without progression rose from 16.3 to 24.9 months (36% lower risk of progression or death), with more liver-enzyme and diarrhea events. *Source:* Labiotech.
- **Roche/Genentech's Tecentriq — FDA approval for stage 3 colon cancer with defective DNA repair (mismatch repair-deficient), after surgery.** In the ATOMIC trial, 86.3% were disease-free at 3 years versus 76.2% on chemotherapy alone, with more severe side effects (84.1% vs 71.9%). *Source:* Labiotech.
- **ImmunityBio's Anktiva — expanded approval** to papillary-only bladder cancer that no longer responds to BCG (a bacterial immunotherapy instilled in the bladder). *Source:* Labiotech.
- **Three eye approvals on Oct 16:** Alcon's Brytly (first nonprescription apraclonidine drop for minor eye redness); subcutaneous Enspryng for thyroid eye disease (an autoimmune condition causing bulging, painful eyes), the first at-home treatment and first IL-6 receptor blocker (IL-6 is an inflammation-signaling protein) for it; and Ryzumvi for presbyopia (age-related loss of near vision). *Source:* [Ophthalmology Times (Oct 23)](https://www.ophthalmologytimes.com/view/one-day-three-approvals-fda-expands-options-for-ocular-redness-thyroid-eye-disease-and-presbyopia). Stock moves not reported.

## 3. Business and deals

### Merck KGaA buys Silence Therapeutics for $1.3 billion; shares double
On Oct 19, Merck KGaA (the German company, separate from U.S. Merck) agreed to pay $20.00 cash per share ($1.3 billion), a 130% premium to Friday's close. Silence stock rose about 125%, trading just under the offer. Closing is expected in Q1 2027 pending a shareholder vote and approvals. The prize is divesiran, a *siRNA* drug (small interfering RNA: a short RNA strand that directs cells to destroy the messenger RNA for one protein, so less is made) for polycythemia vera, a bone-marrow cancer-like disorder that overproduces red blood cells, thickening blood and raising clot and stroke risk. The chairman called its Phase 2 data "potentially best-in-class." Silence had only $72.1M in cash at June 30, so a standalone Phase 3 would have needed fresh funding. *Why it matters:* big companies are still paying steep premiums for mid-stage RNA medicines. *Source:* [Tikr (Oct 19)](https://www.tikr.com/blog/silence-therapeutics-soars-125-on-all-cash-1-3-billion-merck-kgaa-buyout-immediate-and-certain). **Stage:** Phase 2 asset; market-relevant.

### Gilead licenses Vir's "masked" T-cell engagers — up to $1.9 billion
On Oct 14, Gilead licensed three solid-tumor drug candidates built on Vir's PRO-XTEN platform: $90M upfront ($65M cash plus $25M equity at a 30% premium), $45M near-term milestones and up to $1.74B later, plus high-single to low-double-digit royalties. A *T-cell engager* is an antibody-like drug with one arm grabbing a cancer cell and the other grabbing an immune T cell, forcing an attack; in solid tumors this has caused toxic off-target damage. Vir's design covers both arms with "masks" that tumor-specific enzymes (proteases) cut off, so the drug turns on only inside tumors. No stock reaction was reported. *Source:* [Fierce Biotech (Oct 14)](https://fiercebiotech.com/biotech/gilead-strikes-19b-oncology-deal-vir-dual-masked-t-cell-engager-technology). **Stage:** preclinical platform.

Close expected by end of 2026. No premium or stock move available. *Source:* [Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/ma/lite/viatris-to-acquire-pacira-biosciences-for-165-billion-bolstering-non-opioid-pain-treatment-portfolio-22115).

## Top takeaways

1. **Personalized cancer vaccines are real.** A 42% lower relapse/death risk in melanoma is the field's first Phase 3 win, though shares fell on high expectations.
2. **RNA medicines keep advancing.** Hibsago (antisense) won FDA approval and Silence's siRNA drug drew a 130% premium buyout.
3. **Rare-disease and "second-chance" drugs are being rewarded:** Disc's stock jumped 39–60% on a repeat Phase 3.
4. **M&A and licensing remain active** ($1B+ deals for Silence, Pacira, plus Gilead–Vir), with buyers favoring de-risked mid- or late-stage assets.
5. **Selective-activation tech is a theme:** masked T-cell engagers aim to make potent immune drugs safe in solid tumors.
6. **Modest effects invite debate:** Rapport's 3.1-point result shows "statistically significant" and "clinically meaningful" are not the same.
