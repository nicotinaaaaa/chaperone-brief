The Nov 1 brief already covered Genmab's petosemtamab, Beam's BEAM-301, Inovio's VOCERYQ, Geron, Summit's biliary data and the Astellas–Audax deal, so those are not repeated except where there is a new development. **Coverage note:** many trade-press sites block automated access, and several search results were headline-only. I include only details I could confirm and say where a number, stock move or outcome was not available. Prices and moves are as reported by the cited source and may be intraday. This is not investment advice.

**Terms used throughout.**
- **Clinical trial phases.** *Phase 1* is a small first-in-human study focused on safety. *Phase 2* looks for early signs the drug works. *Phase 3* is a large study, usually against placebo or standard care, and is the main basis for approval.
- **Topline results** are the first headline numbers a company releases, before full data are presented or peer-reviewed.
- **Primary endpoint** is the main measurement a trial uses to judge success. *Overall response rate (ORR)* is the share of patients whose tumors shrink by a set amount.
- **Accelerated approval** is an FDA route that approves a drug early based on a measurement thought likely to predict real benefit (a "surrogate"), on condition that the company later confirms benefit in a further trial. The FDA can withdraw approval if it fails.
- **PDUFA date** is the FDA's target deadline for deciding on a drug application. A *major amendment* is new information submitted during review that the FDA treats as big enough to extend that deadline (normally by three months).
- **BLA (Biologics License Application)** is the formal request to the FDA to approve a biologic (a drug made from living cells or biological material, such as an antibody or gene therapy). *Priority review* shortens the FDA's target review time to about 6 months for drugs that could be significant improvements.
- **REMS (Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy)** is a set of extra safety rules the FDA can require, such as special monitoring or restricted prescribing.
- **Bispecific antibody** is an engineered antibody with two different "grabbing" arms that can bind two different targets at once.
- **Gene therapy** delivers genetic material into cells to treat disease, usually by carrying a working gene or a gene-silencing instruction inside a harmless virus.
- **Clinical hold** is an FDA order pausing a trial, usually over a safety concern.
- **Upfront payment, milestones, royalties.** In a licensing deal these are, respectively, money paid at signing, money paid only if goals (trial results, approvals) are met, and a percentage of future sales.
- **Hedge language on deals.** "Reported" deals not confirmed by the companies may never happen.

## 1. Regulatory decisions

### FDA grants accelerated approval to subcutaneous Rybrevant (amivantamab) for recurrent head and neck cancer
**What happened.** On Nov 5, the FDA announced accelerated approval of Rybrevant Faspro (amivantamab plus hyaluronidase-lpuj, a version given as an under-the-skin injection) for adults with recurrent or metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma whose cancer worsened after platinum chemotherapy and an anti-PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy. In the OrigAMI-4 study (Phase 1b/2; first cohort of 102 patients), 42% of patients had their tumors shrink (95% CI 32%–52%): 15% complete responses and 28% partial responses. 56% of responders had a response lasting over 6 months. The source did not report survival or any hazard ratio. The most common side effects were rash (79%), fatigue (39%), nail toxicity (35%), mouth sores (32%) and swelling (28%). Adverse events led to death in 6 patients; the FDA said no new safety signals appeared beyond the drug's known profile. The company/stock reaction was not available.

**Biology.** Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma is cancer of the flat lining cells of the mouth, throat, voice box and nearby areas, linked to tobacco, heavy alcohol use and the virus HPV. It causes pain, trouble swallowing or speaking, and a neck lump; once it has returned or spread it is rarely curable. Amivantamab is a bispecific antibody that blocks two proteins that tumors use to get growth signals: EGFR (a surface receptor many tumors depend on) and MET (a second receptor that lets tumors escape EGFR blockade). Hyaluronidase is an enzyme that helps the injected liquid spread through tissue so a large dose can be given under the skin instead of by IV drip.

**Why it matters.** Few options exist after immunotherapy and chemotherapy have failed. Because this is an accelerated approval based on tumor shrinkage in a study with no comparison group, a confirmatory trial must still show survival benefit. It also adds context to last week's Genmab result: a different EGFR-targeting bispecific (petosemtamab) is being tested earlier in the same disease.
**Source:** [OncLive (Nov 5, 2026)](https://www.onclive.com/view/fda-approves-amivantamab-and-hyaluronidase-for-metastatic-hnscc). **Stage:** approved (conditional); market-relevant.

### FDA tentatively approves the first generic of Gilead's twice-yearly HIV-prevention shot
**What happened.** On Nov 2, the FDA gave tentative approval to a generic version of lenacapavir (Gilead's brand: Yeztugo) made by India's Dr. Reddy's Laboratories, in an arrangement brokered by Unitaid, the Clinton Health Access Initiative and Wits RHI. It is expected to cost about $40 per person per year in 120 low- and middle-income countries, versus a U.S. list price of $28,218 a year for Yeztugo. "Tentative" means the generic cannot be sold in the U.S. while Gilead's patents stand; supply to some countries could begin within weeks, though some require their own approval first.

**Biology.** Lenacapavir is a long-acting HIV *capsid inhibitor*: it blocks the protein shell that HIV uses to protect and deliver its genetic material, stopping the virus from replicating. Used as *PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis)*, meaning medicine taken by HIV-negative people to prevent infection, one injection every six months replaces a daily pill. Two studies in high-risk people found it nearly eliminated new infections. People must test HIV-negative before each injection; side effects include injection-site reactions, headache and nausea. HIV is a virus that destroys immune cells and, untreated, causes AIDS (severe immune failure); the WHO reports 41 million people living with HIV and 570,000 deaths last year.

**Why it matters.** The roughly 700-fold price gap shows how licensing can open access in poorer countries; about 20 million people are estimated to need PrEP, though funding to purchase it is still needed. Commercially, it signals that Gilead's brand revenue will be concentrated in wealthy markets.
**Source:** [WAFB/AP (Nov 2, 2026)](https://www.wafb.com/2026/11/02/fda-approves-more-affordable-version-hiv-prevention-shot/). **Stage:** regulatory (tentative); market-relevant.

### Agios' mitapivat in sickle cell disease: FDA decision delayed three months (update to last week's pending item)
**What happened.** Agios announced on Oct 30 that the FDA moved its decision date from Nov 1, 2026 to Feb 1, 2027. The FDA had asked whether mitapivat will need a REMS; Agios replied on Oct 19 with about nine months of extra safety data, which the FDA treated as a major amendment. Agios says it saw no liver injury in sickle cell patients like that seen in adult thalassemia trials, says the benefit-risk profile is unchanged, and calls itself "launch-ready." Labeling talks, including possible REMS elements, continue. The stock reaction was not available.

**Biology.** Sickle cell disease is an inherited blood disorder in which faulty hemoglobin (the oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells) makes the cells stiff and crescent-shaped. They block small vessels, causing severe pain crises, organ damage and stroke, and they die early, causing anemia. Mitapivat is a pill that activates pyruvate kinase, an enzyme that supplies red blood cells with energy. Thalassemia is another inherited anemia in which the body makes too little hemoglobin.

**Why it matters.** A delay is not a rejection, but a possible REMS (extra safety restrictions) could make launch harder; the liver-safety question is the thing to watch.
**Source:** [HCPLive (Oct 30, 2026)](https://www.hcplive.com/view/mitapivat-pdufa-date-for-sickle-cell-disease-extended-to-february-2027). **Stage:** regulatory delay; market-relevant.

### uniQure's Huntington's gene therapy: FDA accepts application with priority review
**What happened.** Reported Nov 2: the FDA accepted uniQure's BLA for AMT-130 (ifezuntirgene inilparvovec) with priority review and a target decision date of May 1, 2027. NeurologyLive reports 36-month Phase 1/2 high-dose data showing a 75% slowing of disease progression on the cUHDRS (a composite score of movement, thinking and daily function) versus a matched outside control group from the Enroll-HD registry (p=0.003). A separate weekly summary I read cited a 48-month figure of 44% slowing that was not statistically significant. I could not reconcile the two numbers, so treat both with caution. uniQure also agreed with the FDA on a ~200-patient confirmatory trial (2:1 randomization to treatment vs standard of care; Total Functional Capacity at 36 months as the main endpoint), with U.S. screening planned by end of 2026. One weekly roundup showed QURE down about 12.6% over Oct 30–Nov 6; the cause was not given.

**Biology.** Huntington's disease is an inherited, progressive brain disorder caused by a repeat of the DNA letters CAG in the huntingtin gene, producing a toxic protein that kills neurons. It causes involuntary movements, mood changes and cognitive decline, usually starting in midlife and leading to death over roughly 15–20 years; about 75,000 people are affected in the U.S., EU and UK, and no approved drug slows it. AMT-130 is a one-time gene therapy that carries a microRNA (a short RNA that switches off a gene) into the brain's striatum by MRI-guided neurosurgery to lower production of the toxic protein.

**Why it matters.** It would be the first disease-modifying treatment for Huntington's, but the evidence so far compares treated patients with an external control group rather than a placebo group, which regulators view skeptically; the earlier FDA reversal (shares crashed ~40% when the FDA rejected an early-approval path) shows how fragile this has been.
**Sources:** [NeurologyLive (Nov 2, 2026)](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/fda-grants-priority-review-to-huntington-disease-gene-therapy-bla); [Biotech Breakdowns weekly recap](https://biotechbreakdowns.substack.com/p/weekly-news-re-cap-a48). **Stage:** regulatory review; market-relevant.

## 2. Clinical trials

### InnoCare's orelabrutinib succeeds in a Phase 3 trial in immune thrombocytopenia (ITP)
**What happened.** Announced Nov 5: in a 196-adult, placebo-controlled trial, 32.8% of patients on orelabrutinib 50 mg had a durable platelet response, about 25.5 percentage points above placebo (p=0.0001). "Durable" meant platelets of at least 50 (×10⁹/L) at 4 of 6 visits in the last 12 weeks without rescue treatment. Median time to first response was 14 days vs 42 days on placebo; benefit appeared by day 7. Side effects were comparable to placebo, mostly mild (grade 1–2). Full data will be presented at the ASH meeting on Dec 14. Share reaction not available.

**Biology.** ITP is an autoimmune disease in which the immune system destroys platelets, the blood cells that help clotting. Low counts cause easy bruising, nosebleeds, and, in severe cases, dangerous bleeding; the company cites over 200,000 new cases a year worldwide. Orelabrutinib is an oral BTK inhibitor: BTK (Bruton's tyrosine kinase) is an enzyme inside immune B cells that helps them mature and make antibodies, so blocking it dampens the antibody-driven attack on platelets.

**Why it matters.** A placebo-controlled win, with a fast onset, supports BTK inhibition as a pill option for ITP; the reported trial population was previously treated patients. Details of where it was run were not given.
**Source:** [GlobeNewswire/InnoCare (Nov 5, 2026)](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/05/3396134/0/en/2026-ash-oral-presentation-innocare-announces-phase-iii-results-of-orelabrutinib-in-itp-study-meet-primary-endpoint.html). **Stage:** Phase 3, positive; company-reported.

### Other readouts (grouped; all from the Biotech Breakdowns recap, Oct 29–Nov 6)
- **Vanda's tradipitant against GLP-1 nausea (Phase 3).** In patients started on semaglutide (Wegovy/Ozempic) without gradual dose increases, vomiting-plus-nausea occurred in 33.1% on tradipitant vs 50.6% on placebo (p=0.002), a 35% relative reduction. GLP-1 drugs mimic a gut hormone to cut appetite and blood sugar but often cause nausea. Tradipitant blocks the NK1 receptor, a brain signal involved in vomiting. Vanda plans a supplemental application. **Stage:** Phase 3, positive.
- **MetaVia's DA-1726 (Phase 1).** In 9 patients at 48 mg, average weight loss was 6.1% at day 26 and 9.1% at day 54, with improvement in liver fat on exploratory scans. A Phase 2 liver trial is planned next year. Shares rose 27.7% over the week. **Stage:** Phase 1, tiny sample.
- **Tiziana's intranasal foralumab in secondary progressive multiple sclerosis (MS).** The 50 mcg dose reduced microglial activation (immune-cell inflammation in the brain, measured by TSPO PET scan) vs placebo (p=0.0168). MS is an autoimmune disease in which the immune system damages the insulation on nerves, causing numbness, weakness, vision and walking problems; secondary progressive MS is the stage where disability worsens steadily. Foralumab is an antibody against CD3, a protein on T cells. Shares fell 13.9% over the week; the cause was not given. **Stage:** Phase 2-type imaging data, early.
- **Pfizer drops two programs:** PF-07868489 (a Phase 2 treatment for pulmonary arterial hypertension, high blood pressure in the lung arteries) for business reasons, and PF-08654696, an early Metsera GIPR agonist (GIPR is a gut hormone receptor involved in appetite and insulin).
- **Safety flags:** BMS disclosed more detail on a patient death in a CAR-T trial for multiple sclerosis (CAR-T engineers a patient's own T cells to attack a target); Gilead's Kite unit has a partial FDA clinical hold on two trials of a CAR-T (KITE-753) in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, an aggressive blood cancer. Reasons for the hold were not given.

## 3. Business, deals and markets

### AstraZeneca reportedly bids ~$10 billion for Rhythm Pharmaceuticals; shares jump
**What happened.** On Nov 4, the Wall Street Journal reported AstraZeneca made a roughly $10 billion offer (about $124 per share) in October for Rhythm. Neither company has confirmed or commented. Rhythm shares rose about 28% to just over $110 from about $95 (company value roughly $7.6 billion before the jump), and +21% over the week to Nov 6. Analysts at Jefferies, Leerink and William Blair flagged a possible bidding war. AstraZeneca also announced a $1.5 billion annual cost-savings program through 2029.

**Biology.** Rhythm sells a treatment for *acquired hypothalamic obesity*, a rare condition in which damage to the hypothalamus (the brain region regulating hunger and energy use), from a tumor, surgery or injury, causes intense hunger and rapid weight gain. Such drugs act on the melanocortin-4 receptor, a brain pathway that signals fullness. The same week Rhythm raised its LG Chem licensing deal for bivamelagon (a pill candidate) from $305 million to $855 million total, and secured a term loan of up to $750 million.

**Why it matters.** It would be a large bet on rare, brain-driven obesity separate from the crowded GLP-1 field. The offer is unconfirmed, so shares already price in a deal that may not happen.
**Sources:** [investingLive (Nov 4, 2026)](https://investinglive.com/stocks/astrazeneca-makes-10-billion-offer-for-obesity-drugmaker-rhythm-wsj-reports/); [Biotech Breakdowns](https://biotechbreakdowns.substack.com/p/weekly-news-re-cap-a48). **Stage:** reported M&A; market-relevant.

## Top takeaways

1. **The FDA is leaning on conditional pathways, and also delaying.** Rybrevant Faspro won accelerated approval on tumor shrinkage alone, while Agios' sickle cell drug was pushed three months over a possible REMS and uniQure's Huntington's file rests on external-control comparisons. Expect more "approved, but prove it later" outcomes and more safety-driven delays.
2. **EGFR-targeting bispecifics are building a head-and-neck cancer franchise.** Combined with last week's Genmab result, two different EGFR antibodies are now being positioned from first-line to post-immunotherapy.
3. **Obesity money keeps flowing, but into new corners.** A reported $10 billion AstraZeneca bid for rare-obesity specialist Rhythm, Novo's third outlook raise on the Wegovy pill, and Lilly's telehealth push sit alongside Pfizer pruning its early GIPR programs and Vanda testing a drug for the nausea GLP-1s cause.
4. **Global access is being negotiated at the patent line.** A $40-a-year generic lenacapavir against a $28,000 U.S. price is a template for two-tier pricing in HIV prevention.
5. **Cuts and cancelled partnerships continue for weaker assets** (Ultragenyx −20%, Novartis–BioArctic ended, Pfizer drops), while gene and cell therapies face safety scrutiny (BMS death, Gilead/Kite hold).
6. **The macro backdrop was shaky** (Nasdaq-100 about −5% for the week, VIX up sharply), yet healthcare held flat, and stock-specific catalysts such as M&A rumors and positive data drove the biggest biotech moves.
