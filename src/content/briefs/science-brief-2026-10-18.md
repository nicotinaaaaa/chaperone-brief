This brief covers biology, biotech and pharma news from roughly the past 10 days. Every disease and technical term is explained at first use. **Coverage note:** many trade-press sites block automated access, and several articles gave no numbers, so I include only details I could confirm and say where something (such as a stock move or a statistic) was not available. Items covered in the Oct 11 brief (ArriVent, Endeavor, Ono–Biohaven, Nobel prizes, etc.) are not repeated.

**Terms used throughout.** A *clinical trial* tests a drug in people: **Phase 1** (small group, mainly safety), **Phase 2** (early evidence it works), **Phase 3** (large, usually compared with placebo or standard care; the main basis for approval). A *primary endpoint* is the single main measurement a trial is designed to prove. *Placebo* is a dummy treatment. *Progression-free survival (PFS)* is how long patients live without their cancer growing; *overall survival (OS)* is how long they live. *Statistically significant* means the difference is unlikely to be down to chance. A *biomarker* is a measurable signal in the body (such as a protein level) used as a stand-in for disease activity. The *FDA* is the U.S. drug regulator; the *EMA* is Europe's, and its committee, the *CHMP*, votes on whether to recommend a drug for approval (the European Commission then formally approves, usually within a couple of months).

## 1. Clinical trials

### Bristol Myers' lung-scarring pill admilparant works at a low dose but not a high one
**What happened.** On Oct 12, Bristol Myers Squibb (BMS) said its pill admilparant met the main goal of the Phase 3 ALOFT-IPF trial: over one year, it slowed the decline in lung function compared with placebo. But only the 60 mg dose clearly worked; the higher 120 mg dose showed an effect that was not statistically significant. No effect sizes or p-values were released. Analysts called the missing "more drug, more effect" pattern "confounding" (RBC) but said the data might still support approval (Leerink). BMS stock jumped more than 6% pre-market, then reversed to about a 4% loss; Contineum Therapeutics, which has a rival drug of the same type in Phase 2, fell about 30%.

**Biology.** Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a disease where lung tissue scars and stiffens for no known reason, so breathing gets harder over time; it mostly affects people over 60 and survival is often only a few years. Lung function is tracked by *forced vital capacity* (FVC) — the amount of air you can blow out after a deep breath. Admilparant is an *LPA1 receptor antagonist*: it blocks a docking site (receptor) on cells for a signaling fat called lysophosphatidic acid, which is thought to drive scar formation.

**Why it matters.** Existing IPF drugs (Esbriet, Ofev, Jascayd) have limits, and BMS is reportedly eyeing filings. But earlier drugs of this class have failed: a BMS predecessor was stopped for liver toxicity and Amgen cancelled one after a Phase 2 miss, and BMS changed liver-safety monitoring in Europe last month, so the safety data are the thing to watch.
**Source:** BioPharma Dive (Oct 12, 2026). **Stage:** Phase 3, mixed-positive; market-relevant.

### BioNTech's mRNA head-and-neck cancer therapy misses an interim goal
**What happened.** On Oct 13, BioNTech said BNT113 plus Merck's Keytruda did not extend progression-free survival versus Keytruda alone at an interim check in first-line HPV16-positive head and neck cancer. An independent monitoring committee advised continuing so the trial can test its second main goal, overall survival. BioNTech shares fell about 6% in early trading. PFS numbers were not disclosed. A Leerink analyst said she had expected a negative result because prior evidence was "sparse."

**Biology.** Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma is cancer of the lining of the mouth, throat or voice box, causing sores, pain swallowing or hoarseness. Some cases are caused by the HPV16 virus (human papillomavirus type 16, also behind most cervical cancer). BNT113 is an *mRNA immunotherapy*: it delivers genetic instructions (mRNA, the temporary "recipe" cells read to make proteins) for HPV16 viral proteins so the immune system learns to attack tumor cells carrying them. Keytruda is a *checkpoint inhibitor*, an antibody that removes a brake (PD-1) that tumors use to hide from immune cells.

**Why it matters.** It is another setback for mRNA cancer treatments after a colorectal trial failure earlier this year, and BioNTech is leaning more on a different drug, pumitamig (a *bispecific antibody*, which grabs two targets at once).
**Source:** BioPharma Dive (Oct 13, 2026). **Stage:** Phase 2/3, interim miss; market-relevant.

### argenx stops a Sjögren's trial for futility and its stock falls 11–15%
**What happened.** On Oct 8, an independent monitoring board recommended halting argenx's Phase 3 UNITY trial of Vyvgart in Sjögren's disease after an interim analysis. *Futility* means the board judged the trial very unlikely to reach its goal if continued. Shares fell about 15% on the day per BioPharma Dive (TIKR reported 11%), leaving a market value near $50 billion. Analysts had pegged the use at up to $1.4 billion peak sales. Separately, argenx said its celiac candidate FB102 (acquired via a $2.2 billion Forte Biosciences deal) prevented gluten-induced gut damage in a Phase 2 study.

**Biology.** Sjögren's disease is an autoimmune disease (the immune system attacks the body's own tissue) mainly hitting tear and saliva glands, causing dry eyes and mouth, fatigue and joint pain; it affects mostly women. Vyvgart works by lowering IgG, the common antibody type, including the harmful self-attacking versions. Celiac disease is an autoimmune reaction to gluten that damages the small intestine.

**Why it matters.** Vyvgart's growth story depends on expanding into more autoimmune diseases; this is a notable stumble.
**Sources:** BioPharma Dive (Oct 8); TIKR; Morningstar/Dow Jones. **Stage:** Phase 3 failure; market-relevant.

### Arrowhead's RNA drug cuts tau by ~73% in healthy volunteers
**What happened.** On Oct 14, Arrowhead reported interim Phase 1/2a data for ARO-MAPT, a gene-silencing injection. In healthy volunteers, average tau protein in spinal fluid fell 40% (2.1 mg/kg), 69% (6.4 mg/kg) and 73% (12.8 mg/kg) by day 105, 90 days after the last dose in the top group. The drug was described as generally well tolerated with no serious events. Patients with early Alzheimer's are still being enrolled. No stock reaction was reported in the source. Group sizes and p-values were not provided.

**Biology.** *Tau* is a brain protein that, in Alzheimer's and related "tauopathies," misfolds and clumps inside nerve cells, which then die; Alzheimer's disease causes progressive memory loss and confusion, usually after age 65, and is fatal. *RNA interference (RNAi)* uses short RNA molecules to tell cells to destroy the messenger RNA for one specific protein, so less is made. Here the target is the *MAPT* gene that encodes tau. CSF (cerebrospinal fluid, the fluid around brain and spine) tau is an exploratory biomarker.

**Why it matters.** Showing an RNAi drug can reach the brain and strongly lower tau, with possible quarterly dosing, is a key proof-of-concept — though lowering a biomarker in healthy people does not show it helps patients.
**Sources:** Pharmacally; BioPharma Dive (Oct 14, 2026). **Stage:** early clinical (Phase 1/2a).

### Entrada's Duchenne exon-skipping therapy shows a small dystrophin gain
**What happened.** On Oct 14, Entrada reported results from the first cohort (8 patients, ages 4–20, randomized 3:1 to drug or placebo) of its Phase 1/2 study of ENTR-601-45 at an initial 5 mg/kg dose. Dystrophin rose a mean 0.10 percentage points (nominal p=0.03, six treated patients); placebo fell 0.02. No functional benefit was expected or seen. Headache was the most common side effect; one patient had two serious events (vomiting, fever) that resolved. A 10 mg/kg cohort is dosing now, with data in the first half of 2027.

**Biology.** Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a genetic disease, mostly in boys, in which a faulty *dystrophin* gene leaves muscle cells without the protein that protects them, causing progressive weakness, loss of walking in the teens and early death from heart or breathing failure. *Exon skipping* uses a short genetic patch to make cells read past a faulty section of the gene so some working dystrophin is produced; Entrada attaches the patch to a *cell-penetrating peptide* to help it enter muscle.

**Why it matters.** The gain is tiny, so the real test is the higher doses; existing exon-skippers also produce modest dystrophin.
**Source:** Entrada release via Finviz/RTTNews (Oct 14, 2026). **Stage:** early clinical.

## 2. Regulatory decisions

- **Revolution Medicines' daraxonrasib (Rasonque) — positive EU review (Oct 15).** The CHMP recommended approval for previously treated metastatic pancreatic cancer (cancer that has spread; pancreatic adenocarcinoma is aggressive and often found late). The drug is a *RAS inhibitor*: RAS is a growth-signal protein mutated in many cancers and long considered "undruggable"; this pill glues RAS to another protein (cyclophilin A) to disable it. The FDA already approved it Aug 26, 2026, more than six months early, after a Phase 3 that roughly doubled overall survival (absolute figures were not given). *Sources:* RTTNews (Oct 15); Fierce Pharma (Aug 26).
- **Ionis' Tryngolza (olezarsen) — CHMP backs it for severe hypertriglyceridemia (Oct 16).** This is a condition of very high triglycerides (blood fats) that raises the risk of pancreatitis (painful, sometimes dangerous inflammation of the pancreas). Olezarsen is an *antisense drug*, a short strand of genetic material that blocks production of a specific protein (here one that slows triglyceride clearance). European Commission approval is pending. *Source:* RTTNews.
- **Omeros stock falls ~12% (Oct 16)** after the CHMP reaffirmed a negative opinion on narsoplimab for transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy (damaging clots in small blood vessels after a bone marrow transplant). *Source:* RTTNews.
- **J&J's Talvey combination — CHMP backs earlier-line multiple myeloma (Oct 16).** Multiple myeloma is cancer of antibody-making plasma cells in bone marrow, causing bone damage, anemia and kidney problems. Talvey is a *bispecific antibody* that links a myeloma-cell marker (GPRC5D) to immune T cells. *Source:* RTTNews.
- **Other approvals (Oct 16, FDA):** UCB's Zilbrysq autoinjector for self-administration (for myasthenia gravis, an autoimmune disease causing muscle weakness) and Viatris' Ryzumvi for presbyopia (age-related loss of near vision). No stock moves reported.

## 3. Business and deals

### Biotech IPO window stays open: City Therapeutics (+24%) and Iambic (+18%)
In the week of Oct 12, City Therapeutics raised $200M in an upsized IPO (a company's first public stock sale) at roughly a $1.0B valuation, closing up 24% on day one; it develops RNAi drugs, led by CITY-FXI, which targets Factor XI, a clotting protein, for blood-clot diseases. Iambic Therapeutics raised $150M (about $809M valuation), rose 18% on day one and was up about 49% by Oct 16; it uses AI-guided design of small-molecule cancer drugs. Both are Phase 1 companies. *Why it matters:* investors are paying up for early-stage biotech again. *Source:* Renaissance Capital (Oct 16). **Stage:** market-relevant.

### AstraZeneca–Insitro AI deal; Labcorp buys BioReference assets
Insitro receives $40M upfront (more if goals are met) to use machine learning to find treatments for MASH (liver inflammation and scarring caused by fat buildup) and cardiomyopathies (diseases of the heart muscle) (RTTNews, Oct 15). Labcorp will acquire BioReference Health assets, and OPKO shares rose 7.35% (RTTNews, Oct 15); terms were not available to me. **Stage:** platform / market-relevant.

## 4. Basic science

- **A "brain gene" jumped into a virus (Science, reported Oct 7–8).** Cornell's Cedric Feschotte and colleagues found two copies of human BC200 DNA inside the molluscum contagiosum virus (a common skin virus). BC200 is a gene that makes a short RNA rather than a protein and is abundant in neurons; it started as a "jumping gene" (a DNA sequence that copies itself around the genome) 35–55 million years ago and kept the ability to move, likely borrowing the machinery of another jumping gene, LINE-1. Among 908 people, eight person-to-person-varying BC200 insertions were found. It is not known what the viral copies do. *Source:* ScienceAlert (Oct 8).
- **Octopus genome rearrangement (Nature Communications, ScienceDaily Oct 12).** University of Vienna researchers mapped the 3D folding of DNA in octopuses, squid and cuttlefish and found that an ancient large-scale reshuffling put distant genome regions in contact, near genes for nervous-system development. This may help explain their complex brains but does not prove cause. *Source:* ScienceDaily.
- **Public-health note: Ebola in Kenya (Oct 14–16).** Kenya confirmed a second case of Bundibugyo ebolavirus (a rarer Ebola species causing fever and often severe bleeding illness; no licensed vaccine was mentioned in the source) in a family contact of the first patient, who died Oct 5 after travelling from the Democratic Republic of Congo. The new patient was already in quarantine and officials report no wider community spread. *Sources:* The Star/Reuters (Oct 15); RTTNews (Oct 16).

## Top takeaways

1. **Mixed results are the norm in late-stage trials.** BMS's dose puzzle and BioNTech's interim miss show how often big programs deliver partial or unclear answers.
2. **Autoimmune expansion is not guaranteed.** argenx's Sjögren's halt cost it 11–15% in a day and reminds investors that a drug that works in one disease may fail in another.
3. **RNA medicines are moving into the brain.** Arrowhead's tau-lowering data, if confirmed in patients, would broaden RNAi beyond liver and blood targets.
4. **RAS inhibition is becoming real medicine.** Daraxonrasib won an early U.S. approval and a positive EU opinion for a very hard cancer.
5. **The IPO window is open.** City and Iambic both traded well above their offer prices.
6. **Small tail risks remain:** the Kenya Ebola cluster is contained so far but worth tracking.
